## Agreed product direction

HardHit's main mode remains the six-hitting contest: six legal deliveries per side, biggest six wins, and a wicket does not end a regular innings. Make the underlying ball, contact, decisions and controls more credible while retaining those explicit contest rules. Do not silently replace them with conventional team innings or add a separate mode as a prerequisite.

**Core requirement:** realistic cricket logic and physics are the default for the whole game, not an optional feature or mode. Do not trade correctness of the underlying decisions or physics for device performance or a simplified scoring model. Where evidence is missing, make the uncertainty and remaining acceptance gate explicit rather than calling an approximation realistic. Graphics quality and rendering effects should adapt to device capability while preserving the same gameplay simulation and decisions.

- Aim for broad web-device support, prioritizing Android and iPhone optimization and testing first, while maintaining desktop keyboard and browser support.
- Assistance is an on/off setting whose strength depends on the main player's level. Its level mapping, progression and exact effects still need to be designed and tested. It may help the player understand or execute controls, but must not change the underlying physics, laws, scoring or event results for the same executed inputs. Disclose any transformation from intended input to executed input and make unassisted behavior measurable.
- Ask new players during setup whether to enable assistance. Main-player level should reflect both match experience and batting and bowling performance; the exact formula remains to be designed. Level should also improve batter and bowler attributes within bounded, realistic ranges, independently of assistance. Save progression on the device across offline matches. Attribute improvements represent the player's capabilities within the same underlying simulation, not a separate physics or scoring model.
- For development and testing, provide a player-level picker dropdown so reviewers can try different main-player levels without earning them. The assistance curve (including whether strength increases or decreases with level) remains undecided; the picker chooses a test player level, not a committed assistance policy. Test overrides must be clearly labeled, isolated from saved progression and unavailable in normal release play unless deliberately enabled for development.
- Introduce automatic fielders and running first, then offer players both automatic running and a choice to run. In manual running, a poor decision can result in a run-out, determined by ball, fielder and crease timing. Plan manual fielding separately if later desired.
- For phone batting, let the player select shot and direction, then time the swing.
- Improve the offline AI first. Online matches are a later, separately scoped decision, not a dependency for offline improvements.
- Keep the current longest-six winner and tie-break rules; a wicket does not end the innings before all six legal balls are completed. Preserve existing saved data and distinguish a playability approximation from a calibrated measurement. Camera batting remains outside this plan.
- Follow applicable ICC playing conditions and MCC Laws for cricket rules except explicit contest overrides. Law 19 defines a boundary six but does not specify a longest-six ranking metric. The agreed contest metric is horizontal carry from the bat–ball contact point to the first landing point, excluding bounce and roll; when a stand or roof interrupts flight, project the uninterrupted landing point. Compare unrounded simulated distances for ranking even when displayed rounded values match, then use the existing tie-breaks if distances are equal. This is a contest rule, not a claim about an ICC distance convention.

## What works well

- Match and practice share important batting resolution and delivery trajectory functions, reducing—but not eliminating—the risk of visual and scoring disagreement.
- The shot flight uses fixed simulation steps; unit cases cover frame-rate damping, bounce and boundary ordering. The rendered pitch, stumps and ball have explicit measured dimensions.
- Extras, free hits, simplified LBW/bowled and innings transitions have meaningful representative unit tests. Practice has isolated progression and supports batting and bowling configurations.
- Keyboard, touch, handedness, pause/rotation, interrupted inputs and callbacks have automated regression coverage. Local storage handles unavailable or malformed data without preventing a match.
- There is a documented physics evidence trail and an explicit description of the contest's nonstandard rules. The code has browser regression scripts and a reproducible production build.

**Phase 0 — Lock the product contract and evidence.** Document the agreed six-hitting contest rules and distance metric above, broad web support with Android/iPhone prioritized, on/off assistance, progression and acceptable input latency. The assistance-to-level mapping remains undecided. Inventory sources, coordinate axes, units and authoritative state; record uncertainty and licensing. Create reproducible seeds and traceable event logs without user video. Acceptance: contest rules and boundary examples reviewed; every later benchmark specifies source, setup, tolerance, seed and held-out data. Automated: schema/units/coordinate round trips, deterministic replay and no regression to the contest.

**Phase 1 — Make a six reliably testable and harden state transitions.** Investigate the failed strict match with recorded input timestamps, selected shot, contact quality, launch velocity, crossing/bounce and final score. First demonstrate a deterministic six through the full input-to-score path and a negative control; keep the strict assertion. Define exactly when an outcome is provisional/final, isolate each delivery by generation, and make resolution idempotent. Acceptance: a known achievable six, bounced four, miss, no-ball hit and interruption each display and score the same outcome, once. Automated: seeded full matches in both orders, strict six, duplicate/stale callback and pause/rematch race cases, fixed-seed replays at multiple frame rates. Do not force a six by weakening the test or changing physics before diagnosing the cause.

**Phase 2 — Establish a single event and geometry model.** Define SI units, pitch origin, release/pitch/contact/stump/rope planes, ball radius, model/render scale, coordinate handedness and time source. Trace one delivery through input, ball path, contact decision, outgoing flight, rope and score; resolve whether the preliminary distance should be retained or replaced by a single authoritative flight outcome. Acceptance: independent expected coordinates and event timestamps match the displayed and adjudicated events within declared numerical tolerances; no duplicate scoring. Automated: parameter/property tests for both batting hands and bowling arms, all directions, frame rates, boundaries and invalid inputs; compare game and practice with the same seeded inputs.

**Phase 3 — Calibrate incoming ball physics.** Gather licensed measured pace/spin delivery data with source, units, speed, bounce position, seam/spin, surface, air conditions and uncertainty. Fit a simple transparent model to training measurements, reserve separate deliveries, surfaces and players as holdout. Add realistic release, gravity, air and bounce models only as data justify; reject impossible inputs and expose controlled conditions. Acceptance: predicted time, bounce and post-bounce path fall within *predeclared data-dependent* tolerances on held-out measurements and remain continuous/finite. Automated: trajectory fixtures, conservation/plausibility bounds, bounce ordering, parameter sensitivity, mirrored arm/hand symmetry and regression over 30/60/120 fps. Do not derive a universal coefficient from one study.

**Phase 4 — Model bat–ball contact and outgoing ball.** First define contact geometry for the manual-control game: bat path/face, sweet spot, finite bat extent, ball radius and timing, with a transparent mapping from button input to swing. Use measured bat/ball examples to fit a bounded contact response and output speed/direction/spin, with defensible uncertainty. Keep animation and adjudication synchronized; reserve camera input for its separate plan. Acceptance: a missed swing cannot create a clean hit; center/off-center/edge cases show consistent contact and motion; equivalent mirrored inputs behave symmetrically; held-out measured launch conditions meet predeclared tolerances. Automated: swept collision and tunneling tests, exact grazing/miss cases, timing sweeps, kinematic consistency, direction symmetry, 30/60/120 fps integration and visual/browser comparison. Physical measurements and human review are required for realism claims.

**Phase 5 — Flight, surface and stadium.** Replace fixed assumptions only with calibrated, bounded drag/bounce/rolling behavior, optional spin/lift and ground properties supported by evidence. Make boundary geometry and stand/obstacle collision explicit and share it with rendering and scoring; document distance definition and any cap. Acceptance: analytic no-drag/ballistic controls, calibrated held-out trajectories and correct first-contact/boundary events within chosen numerical/data tolerances. Automated: convergence against smaller timesteps, energy/plausibility checks where applicable, rope/bounce ordering at step edges, all directions, noncircular boundaries, catches near rope when fielders exist, and no tunneling.

**Phase 6 — Rules and adjudication.** Specify which MCC Laws and ICC playing-condition version each mode follows; separate deliberate contest overrides. Implement delivery legality, wides, no-ball priority/free-hit chain, pad/bat contact order, bowled/LBW and available dismissals incrementally, with explicit uncertainty for decisions not modeled. Acceptance: a traceable decision table and fixture for every implemented rule, both hands/arms, impact/pitch zones, edge cases and competing events; explain omitted laws in product copy. Automated: table/property tests, legal-ball accounting, extras and boundary combinations, free-hit sequences, simultaneous contact/boundary cases, state-machine transition and replay invariants. Review the chosen conditions against authoritative current sources before release.

**Phase 7 — Fielding and running.** Preserve the contest rules while replacing distance-only run estimates with automatic fielders, catch outcomes, running decisions and crease timing; add byes/leg byes and run-outs only as the event model supports them. Build automatic running first, then offer a player-choice running option; manual fielding is a separate later choice. Tie outcomes to actual flight and event timing. Acceptance: fielders cannot catch unreachable balls; runner and ball events agree with score in both running options, and ties/over endings follow the published contest rules. Automated: deterministic field placement/trajectory fixtures, spatial interception and race timing boundaries, innings and extras integration, interruption/cancellation and browser end-to-end cases. Tune against independent match data and human testing, not a single scripted target.

**Phase 8 — Offline AI and difficulty calibration.** Centralize injectable seeded randomness, record bot decisions and replace the QA simulator's misleading denominator, offset label and unused mode. Measure distributions over many seeds for both hands, arms, line/length, difficulty and play styles; set confidence intervals and reproducible seeds for failures. Difficulty should control observable bot execution consistency and tactical choice; level-dependent on/off player assistance must operate on guidance or executed input rather than changing the physics or scoring for equivalent executed inputs. Add a clearly labeled development-only player-level dropdown for evaluation, without committing to whether assistance rises or falls with level; design and verify the eventual progression mapping separately. Acceptance: documented monotonic bot difficulty metrics with tolerances chosen before tuning; no unwinnable or always-winning configuration; mirrored batting patterns and wide/no-ball rates checked against chosen design and data. Automated: Monte Carlo with confidence intervals, distribution drift tests, test-level override isolation from saved progression, and deterministic failing-seed replay. Human playtests judge perceived fairness and fun.

**Phase 9 — Playability, feedback and accessibility.** Align timing labels to the same resolved window and disclose when assistance modifies executed input. Keep rules, scoring and physics invariant for identical executed inputs at every assistance setting. Make aiming, line/length, shot selection, delivery timing, outcome and mode rules understandable without relying only on color or sound. Offer assistance on/off, asking new players during setup, and add readable UI, accessible names/focus and interruptible touch/multitouch; preserve a clear unobstructed view of ball and critical controls. Design and validate persistent offline progression that fairly rewards match experience and batting and bowling results, and applies bounded realistic attribute improvements independently of assistance. Acceptance: keyboard and touch users can complete a match, recover from interruption and understand the result; target-device participants complete defined tasks without hidden rules; progression persists across offline matches. Automated: identical-input physics/adjudication invariants across assistance settings; progression, persistence and migration tests; browser keyboard/focus and accessibility checks, touch emulation, varied viewport/zoom/reduced-motion and contrast checks, screenshots and interruption tests. Actual iOS/Android devices and human accessibility review are separate gates.

**Phase 10 — Performance, persistence and release readiness.** Profile production startup, memory, frame pacing, battery/thermals and input latency on a phone-first device/browser matrix with desktop support; track regressions against predeclared budgets. Adapt graphics detail and effects to device capabilities while keeping simulation and adjudication consistent across tiers. Review and update vulnerable tooling with a migration plan and rerun checks; validate asset rights. Add versioned local-save migrations, storage-failure coverage and reproducible match records. If online competition is later approved, design authoritative state, anti-cheat, privacy, reconnect and multiplayer separately. Acceptance: published measured budgets met on the target matrix, identical executed-input outcomes across graphics tiers, clean upgrade/migration and no unresolved critical release blockers. Automated: cross-tier deterministic simulation parity, clean install/build, dependency advisory scan, production browser suite, resource/performance budgets, storage corruption and migration fixtures, CI and deterministic seed replay. Device and security reviews cannot be replaced by unit tests.
